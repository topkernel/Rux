The same probe binary, the same 128MB working set, the same virtio-blk backend; the Rux gap concentrates in the msync/sync phases (the mapped-dirty-page writeback path) and fsync (the journal-commit path) — consistent with the fsync row on the fio page, the storage stack's current main shortfall. The sync-phase 487× is a basis difference: Rux's writeback was not exhausted at msync — the probe basis is shown as measured.

§3The context-switch cost anatomy (cctx segments, a mechanism note)

  • The x86_64 switch machinery ~620 ns (schedule 125 + the GRQ lock wait 102 + pick 96 + the mm switch 202–295 + FPU 34 + FS/GS 54 + GPRs ~22) — Linux's native switch machinery ~910 ns; the "GRQ lock wait" is the KVM tax on cross-vCPU locked instructions (Linux's per-rq lock pays it too — it does not mean the GRQ is slow), while the GRQ's in-lock work is counted separately: ≈60 ns production / 75–80 ns minimal bracket (tree work ~20–30 ns + D-1 bookkeeping). After the fdget borrow + getppid O(1), the pbench p2_ctxsw pipe ping-pong reads 0.090–0.105 µs.
  • The wake-side segments (WK_SEL/WK_ENQ/WK_DISP/IPI_N/IPI_NS) and the GRQ-hold decomposition are on scheduler §8; the NOHZ hostile-load A/B (0/10→11/12) on interrupts & timers §6.
Canonical: docs/development/perf-gate-baseline.md (anchors and bands), docs/development/perf-report.md (the pbench design). The Linux columns of pbench and the flush wall come from the two-sided suite runs (run/bench-pair-1117/, pbench-pair); each row carries its newest measured value.
